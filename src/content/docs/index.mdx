## Course Description

**All milestones in this class build on each other. You can and will reuse work you develop throughout the semester. ;)**

In this in-person research seminar course, we will examine foundational and emerging research in Information Retrieval and Information Extraction within Computer Science. We will be focusing on human-centric systems found on the worl wide web. So we will blend a bit HCI with IR/IE produce research artifacts as a group and as individuals this semester. :)

Are you interested in how large collections of unstructured text are searched, ranked, interpreted, and transformed into structured knowledge or useful insights? Do you want to understand both system-centric and human-centered approaches to Information Retrieval and Information Extraction? Are you interested in reading current research and applying those ideas by reproducing, extending, or critically evaluating existing systems, methods, or datasets? Then this class is for you. ;)

This is a **BYOR (Bring Your Own Research) course**. You will propose, design, implement, and evaluate a research artifact, system, model, etc. of your choosing. You will create tangible artifacts from research as a group and as individuals. Some of you will bring research (i.e., systems, models, studies, etc.) at different levels of progress, that is okay! You will learn how to elicit and incorporate feedback from others to improve your ideas and research. 

We will engage in class discussions on research papers from top Computer Science Conferences, such as SIGIR, CIKM, WSDM, WWW, and CHIIR and some supporting work from CHI and CSCW. The course emphasizes both system-centric and human-centered perspectives, including interaction techniques, retrieval models, ranking, extraction pipelines, data lineage, evaluation methods, natural language processing, and ethical considerations. In part, we will study how large collections of unstructured text (both human- and machine-generated) are searched, ranked, interpreted, and transformed into structured knowledge and human insights.  Application domains include extraction from noisy and user-generated text (i.e, social media and WikiData), benchmark construction from user interactions, and retrieval-augmented systems. Students will also focus on reproducing, extending, or critically evaluating research systems, methods, and datasets.

**Knowledge Prerequisites**
* Advanced proficiency in Python and/or other languages (i.e., Julia, Go, Rust)
* Advanced knowledge of probability, statistics, and information theory
* Prior experience with applied machine learning and deep learning or human-centric methods

**Learning Goals through Milestones and Research**
* Implement core information retrieval data structures and evaluate retrieval and extraction effectiveness using ranked search over a document corpus.		
* Develop a text processing pipeline that applies standard NLP techniques to extract and represent information and insights from unstructured text.		
* Analyze user interaction data and develop user models to improve personalization in retrieval systems.		
* Conduct research by proposing, building, and evaluating a novel Information Retrieval and Extraction system or pipeline.

**In this research seminar**, we will examine foundational and emerging work in Information Retrieval and Information Extraction, including retrieval models, ranking, natural language processing, extraction pipelines, user interaction, evaluation, data lineage, and ethical considerations. Students will read and discuss research papers, analyze research methods and evaluation strategies, and complete a semester research project focused on an Information Retrieval or Information Extraction problem. Some of the research venues we will read papers from include:

* ACM SIGIR (Special Interest Group on Information Retrieval)
* ACM CIKM (Conference on Information and Knowledge Management)
* ACM WSDM (Web Search and Data Mining)
* ACM The Web Conference (WWW)
* ACM CHI (Human Factors in Computing Systems)
* ACM CHIIR (SIGIR Conference on Human Information Interaction and Retrieval)
* ACM CSCW (Computer Supported Cooperative work)
