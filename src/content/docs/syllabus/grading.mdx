## What will you be doing for this class?

### Research Milestones

This course is heavily scaffolded to help guide through the research process to design, implement, and/or evaluate a real-world research artifact or system. The first month is about brainstorming, discussing research ideas, and engaging in some hands-on group work to learn the ropes. This culminates in a proposal. After that, as a class we will discuss and decide on how we will work through building our research artifacts/systems for the rest of the semester. By the end of the class you will have substantial progress on your goals outlined in the proposal and have an artifact/system, final presentation/demo, and a short research paper.

**You will reuse work and improve on it throughout the semester**, so there might seem like a lot of milestones you will be reusing work. For example your (F)inal Research Paper is an edited version of (I)nitial Proposal. Same is true for the presentations. ;) Also, what you develop in (A)bstracts + Ideation will be directly reused in (R)esearch Assignment (Group) and (I)nitial Proposal + Presentation. (C)heck-In Progress on (Semester Assignment), is a one-page document summarizing your progress to date. :)

## Participation & Critical Thinking

### Reading Comments

Reading papers should be done before class the day the reading is due. Please submit each reading comment to GitHub under the date it is due and paper title within "Reading Comments". If there are multiple papers assigned for one day, you need to do the comments for all of the papers. This is because our class meets one day a week.

Each reading comment consists of two parts: 
* First, a novel comment about the research's contribution/finding.
* Second, a novel comment about your assessment of the research. The research assessment could be an area of improvement or future work.

> In total your reading comment should be 3-5 sentences. Being able to communicate briefly and clearly is a valuable skill in research. Please communicate by explaining view points plainly, please avoid using jargon or overly specific terms. Posting overly verbose comments will make it more difficult to earn the full 1 point per comment. I will be randomly checking for AI usage, do not have AI write the comments.

> If you do want to add extra thoughts (i.e., sentences) in your comments to share with the class that is okay. Just do something in your post so I know what is your comment to be graded and label the longer ungraded comment/s as "Extra Reading Comments".

> Your comments can also be in response to existing comments on GitHub for that research. ;)

:::tip[Grading and Extra Credit]
Each GitHub post will be receive a grade of 0, 0.5, or 1 point. Your final grade for Reading Comments is summative. Once you have accumulated 15 points for Reading Comments then you will receive 100% of the 15 points. If you only recieve 10 total points for reading comments throughout the semesyer then your final grade for reading comments will be 10.

> If you earn over 15 points for reading comments, you will earn 6 late hours per 1 extra point from your reading comments.  
:::

### Paper Discussion Leader (2x)

Think of yourself as the moderator for a class discussion for a specific paper. We strongly recommend doing activities and/or slides to help support class discussion. Everyone will be class discussion leader twice:

> You will lead a discussion for one of the papers posted on the course website everyone reads and provides reading comments for. 

> You will also lead the discussion of a paper of your choosing, it must be from one of the CS conferences listed on the homepage and related to the class or about your semester project:
https://github.com/uri-hax/csc592-ire-f26/discussions/5

> Plan for Discussions on your paper to take around 30 minutes.

:::note
Sign-ups will be made available during the semester over GitHub.
:::

:::tip[Some TODOs for being the discussion leader...]
Provide a short summary and opening with questions. Be sure to ask engaging follow-up questions. To excel at this, you should become deeply knowledgeable about the research. Try to explore multiple view points your classmates present. You can also present real world examples or stories related to the research.
:::

:::tip[Please create breakout groups for activities/questions!]
This is to encourage equal participation among the class. As class leader you will:
* Pose at least 2 interesting questions to the class. 
* Ask the class to split into 2-4 person groups. 
* Then students will discuss for 4-5 minutes.
* Finally, each group picks a main representative who will summarize and share the groups insights and views.
:::

##### Tips for being the discussion leader

The best way to go about this is to approach it how you feel you best engage in the class. Being a discussion leader is not easy but can be a lot of fun! :)

Normally, people do not give presentation slides. If you feel like you want to, that is okay. Presenting a summary of some kind is something you should do at the beggining. It will help refresh people's brains and get the ideas flowing.

View yourself as the benevolent moderator. Try to engage students and keep the conversation going and balanced. By balanced, I mean try and provide counter examples so people can see each other's perspective. 

One way to engage students is to ask open ended questions to the class sitting on the other side of the room who have not spoken much yet. Sometimes conversations are focused among specific groups instead of the class as a whole.

In the past sometimes the entire class time is filled naturally by discussion. Other times it naturally comes to a close. Have some fun interesting examples, like papers that build off the paper's ideas. Or you can share a personal story reflecting the research! :)

If you are ambitious, plan a class activity!

:::tip[Some other possible ideas to create an engaging class discussion:]
* Relate this research to your own research projects and ideas. (great way to get feedback!)
* What are some broader impacts or papers this research influenced?
* How might the results of this research impact society long term? 
* Any other interesting or creative ideas. :)
:::

### Class Participation

You will be assessed by your class participation throughout the semester. Focus on being present and engaged in class. Provided timely and engaging commments/responses within the discussions and activities. 

## Late Policy

You know because life happens. The late policy only applies for the final hand-in of the Research Milestones. For example, it does not apply to the reading comments or slides.

:::caution[100 Late Hours for Research Milestones...]
You will have a bank of <b>100 late hours total</b> for the final Research Milestones gradescope hand-ins only. These hours will be automatically applied to late milestones throughout the semester. Each research assignment is worth the same amount towards your final grade. Once your bank is empty, then the normal Late Policy below applies.
> Late hours do not count towards handing in slides.
:::

> The hand-ins on Gradescope close at {dates.gradescope_done}.

All Research Milestones are submitted to Gradescope. It is important to start everything early and to not fall behind. If you do fall behind, the table below shows the current late penalties applied:

| Hours Late | < 12 hrs | 24 hrs | 2 days  | 3 days | 4 days | 5 days | > 5 days |
| - | - | - | - | - | - | - | - |
| Penalty | 5% | 10% | 20% | 30% | 40% | 50% | capped at 50% |

The penalty is applied to the grade you earned. So, if you submit an assignment 3 days late and earn a 90% on it. Your final grade for that assignment would be 90.0 - (90.0 * 0.3) = 63.0.

:::note[We are here to help! :)]
Please contact Professor Wallace over email if you find yourself struggling, so we can seek accommodations.. This could be temporary or lingering health conditions, or life situations that are affecting your learning and/or classroom experience. It is not common that we make exceptions to the late policy. But we want to ensure you learn and complete the course without overwhelming stress.
:::
