### What will you be doing?

You will form into groups for 2-3 possible mini group research assignments, each designed to contribute to an existing paper under revision, a dataset/artifact, or to influence something potentially really coool!

**You will select one of the three research ideas:**

* **Oral History of CS** -> mimic the oral history traditions of ancient Greece, by finding and extracting an oral history of Computer Science.
* **Retrieve-IT: Tabular Data Augmentation from Social Media** (aka build-a-dataset) -> Develop and test an automated version of Retrieve-IT for Social Media extraction to augment an existing Tabular Dataset.
* **Word Difficulty Text Pipeline on Everyday Text**: Identify Difficult Words in Everyday vs Machine Text. Extract these words and run analyses. Is there a difference in human vs machine generated text?

### AI Tools (Jev)

Each of these three research ideas involves retrieving appropriate web pages and then extracting information. For the extraction part, we will use newer systems to automate this process, here are some examples:

Fun quick video:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TbkUKCm3CHQ

* `open jev`: https://openjev.com/
* `jev-ultrafast`: https://github.com/browser-use/jev-ultrafast
    * optional info video (sorry it is AI voice) - but it includes some good examples: http://youtube.com/watch?v=NFKHLhAvj1g
* `jev-search`: https://github.com/superagents-lab/jev-search

In their words, it uses SEMIF, sematic ifs from open models. What's really cool, it runs locally in your browser, and is highly interactive. TypeSafe AI’s Jev is a `System One` model that returns typed decisions and calibrated probabilities rather than text, making it ideal for structured extraction tasks

:::caution[Why JEV?]
JEV is a newer model (from TypeSafe AI), that balances speed vs cost ($) trade-offs. Most importantly, it provides users agency over their actions and costs. Shaun believes that as the costs on larger AI models grow (along with their complexity in terms of UX/DX) that using models like Jev locally will be more attractive in everyday scenarios. There is a long history of people generally picking the convenient option.
:::

### First Steps and What Artifact (code/dataset) Will You Produce?

#### Artifact for Oral History of CS

Mirroring the ancient Greeks, you will create an oral history of Computer Science by extracting information from the World Wide Web: [WayBackMachine](https://web.archive.org/) (faculty websites, articles, etc.), Reddit, papers, videos, etc.

> **Need a list of CS Faculty?** Here is Shaun's system that has a list of CS Faculty from his dissertation: https://drafty.cs.brown.edu/

:::tip[Artifact]
Create an oral history of Computer Science using a metadata markup language [MEDFORD](https://github.com/TuftsBCB/medford) to annotate and add metadata per source.
> **Possible Stretch Goal** create a static HTML page with some preliminary results (consider using automated thematic coding) and observations.
:::

#### Artifact for Retrieve-IT: Tabular Data Augmentation from Social Media

Email Shaun as a group for the latest submission and reviews, and the current repo (ask Shaun for access w/github username):
https://github.com/uri-hax/build-a-dataset

:::tip[Artifact]
Create an automated version of the human-centric retrieval and extraction methods described in the paper.
> **Possible Stretch Goal** run metrics to evaluate the automated method vs the human-method from the paper.
:::

#### Word Difficulty Text Pipeline on Everyday Text

Read the paper that will be published this Fall:
https://shaunwallace.org/files/ASSETS_26__BreakingWordBarriers.pdf

:::tip[Artifact]
Create a chrome extension that can retrieve and extract the seven word-level features from Wikipedia articles and/or Chat-GPT conversationl interfaces.
> **Possible Stretch Goal** actually replace the text with synonyms or similar phrases. ;)
:::

### Short Group Presentations (1 week in & final due date)

These will act as short group presentations to discuss/reflect on what is going on. No slides necessary. Everyone should speak at least once. :)

#### Preliminary Presentation (1 week in) [6 minutes]

Think of your goal for this research project. Whether that includes parts of prototyping a system, creating interactive interfaces, visualizing data, gathering user needs and translating them into designs, finalizing an existing system, and/or evaluating your system with a pilot user study or comparing against perforance benchmarks.

Now you have that scope of work in mind, answer these questions (mentioned above) during the presentation:

```
    % What you want to do/create (and/or) update on your progress
    % Why you want to do it
    % How you plan to do over the next week
    % How will you know if you succeed
    % What are the benefits if the project is successful
```

> If you want add/change goals this is the stage to do it! ;)

### Final Presentation (final due date)

This milestone is short, and meant to simulate the semester project. 

```

```
