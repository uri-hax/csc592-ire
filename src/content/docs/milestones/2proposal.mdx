### What will you be doing?

Creating a proposal for your semester research project modelled after the NSF grant writing process. Do not worry, the presentation is short and the paper is 2 pages. How to pick an idea?

* You will pick a final idea from the first milestone (A), or 
* Continue an idea from (R) the group-based mini-assignment, or 
* Switch over an work on another idea from (R), or 
* Create a whole new one if you have a great idea! :) 

Then, you will start to develop ideas to create a minimally viable set of features/deliverables and possible evaluations to realize your research vision. Ultimatly, you will develop slides to present in class and a short written proposal.

### Paper Proposal: More Details and Guidance

Below are some additional guidelines and thoughts I have written to help you.

#### Paper Proposal: Introduction

The introduction should be fairly straightforward. Feel free to reuse text from your hypothetical abstracts. Be sure to clearly state your research question your system will answer. For the the current state of your system, it is okay if you have nothing right now!

When describing your proposed teck stack (languages, framework, hardware (if any)), be specific! It can be really good to share your experience working with each part of your stack. It would help you understand how feasible your project will be.


#### Paper Proposal: Aim 

Think of your goal for this semester. Whether that includes parts of prototyping a system, creating interactive interfaces, visualizing data, gathering user needs and translating them into designs, finalizing an existing system, and/or evaluating your system with a pilot user study or comparing against perforance benchmarks.

Now you have that scope of work in mind, answer these questions (mentioned above) in writing:

```
    % What you want to do
    % Why you want to do it
    % How you plan to do it
    % How will you know if you succeed
    % What are the benefits if the project is successful
```

:::note[Fun Fact]
When people people write grants (like faculty) we write about 3 aims for a research propsoal. And those 5 questions, are advice I copied from the NSF guidelines for writing grants and proposals. 
:::

#### Paper Proposal: Intellectual Merit & Broader Impacts

What is the potential for the proposed activity to advance knowledge and understanding within its own field or across different fields (Intellectual Merit)?
Address how the proposed activities (Aim) will advance knowledge in your own scientific field and across other fields in creative and transformative ways.

For (Broader Impacts), the NSF (a major grant funding agency) values advancing scientific knowledge and activities that contribute to achieving societally relevant outcomes, or Broader Impacts. Such outcomes include, but are not limited to: 

* the full participation of women, persons with disabilities, and underrepresented minorities in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM); 
* improved STEM education and educator development at any level; 
* increased public scientific literacy and public engagement with science and technology; improved well-being of individuals in society; 
* development of a diverse, globally competitive STEM workforce; 
* increased partnerships between academia, industry, and others; improved national security; increased economic competitiveness of the U.S.; 
* use of science and technology to inform public policy; and enhanced infrastructure for research and education. 

:::note[Fun Fact again...]
These societally relevant outcomes for Broader Impacts listed above, are also from  NSF guidelines for writing grants and proposals. 
:::

## Advice on Presentation and Slides

This handout pulls ideas from this great advice about giving presentations from almost 20 years ago...
[https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/giving-talk.html#slides](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/giving-talk.html#slides)
This was originally written for job talks for CS PhDs, but the advice is fairly universal.

:::note[Be Confident! :)]
Most of all, be confident, be professional, and be your authentic self. This can be challenging to blend, but it will help you be a better presenter.
:::

### What is the best Number and Timing of Slides?
It might be best to give 20 seconds to 1 minute per slide. Quick transition slides, could be around 10 seconds. Do not put too much text on slides! It is difficult to read + listen for most people. ;)

:::danger[Please do not do these things in slides!]
1. read off of slides (we can read and listen)
2. have too much text (it will feel overwhelming)
:::

### How to create good slides?

> We highly recommend using Google Slides or Powerpoint.

Crafting effective slides requires a thoughtful blend of clarity, brevity, and visual appeal. Focus on the main message of each slide. Try to go for one line or pivotal phrases rather than lengthy text blocks. Integrate visuals purposefully to reinforce ideas or concepts, ensuring they complement and enrich the content. 

Create a consistent design across your presentation. Use one font for headers and another for non-header text. 

### How to present?

##### Engage Your Audience Through Eye Contact
Maintain eye contact with your audience to draw them in and assess their engagement and understanding. This interaction helps you gauge their confusion or loss of interest, indicating if your pacing needs adjustment.

##### Stand Tall and Face Your Audience
Ensure you stand and face the audience during your presentation. Standing adds vigor and dynamism to your talk, making it easier to maintain eye contact and connect with your audience. Avoid facing the screen, which could hinder audience feedback and cause communication barriers.

##### Stay Dynamic Without Pacing
While being animated is beneficial, avoid pacing as it can be distracting and give the impression of nervousness or unprofessionalism. Instead, use gestures and movement to enhance your presentation's dynamism.

##### Effective Slide Pointing Techniques
When referencing slides, avoid pointing directly at your laptop screen, as it obstructs the audience's view. Opt for a laser pointer or dynamic hand gestures near the screen to guide attention effectively. Ensure physical contact or proximity to the slide content for clarity.

##### Managing Nervousness and Challenges
Practice more to reduce nervous tics like saying "um" frequently. If flustered during your presentation, take a moment to regroup or skip over challenging sections. Utilize a drink of water to cover pauses or regain composure, ensuring a smoother delivery.
