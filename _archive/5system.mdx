### What will you be doing?

Create your system or build-on your existing system. You can also build extensions or piggy back off of popular systems. For example, Reddit or Wikipedia. This will feature in-class working sessions and weekly self-directed goals.

Phase 1 will be completed in the first week. Phases 2 will be repeated throughout the rest of the semester. ;)

### GenAI Usage

**You may...**
* Use genAI to help brainstorm ideas.
* Provide feedback on your ideas.
* You can use genAI to help plan your study design and help with a hypothetical analysis.
* Use it to make up details/data for your user data.
* Get ideas for other papers to read.
* Find more concise ways to phrase sentences. (basic grammar editing)

**You may not...**
* Use genAI to generate text for your abstract/s.
* Use genAI to shorten text you have written.
* Should not use it to summarize what a research paper says.

## Phase 2: In-Class Working Sessions (weeks 2--N)

This is how we will progress throughout the semester. In part, we are simulating a small team working environment. This outline and method of working is based on your feedback and thoughts as we discussed in class. That way we develop a method of working that matches the team, rather than me enforcing a specific way to do everything. Which is something I found in industry does not always produce the best results.

### Bi-Weekly Check-Ins

These will take every two weeks, they begin during class Oct 16th.

### Stand-ups: Verbal Status Updates

The in-class check-in will be similar to a 1 minute verbal stand-up from Agile methodologies

:::tip[Goals]
Per stand-up you will:
* Say what you accomplished the past 2 weeks.
* Describe what you plan to do the next 2 weeks.
* Describe what/if you need help on.
:::

Keep it short and sweet! Imagine running into your boss on an elevator and give a status update of what you are doing.

###  Managing and Completing Tasks in GitHub

Create tasks in the GitHub project you created for your repo. This is how you will organize your thoughts and enable others to collaborate with you.

:::tip[Goals]
Throughout the semester:
* Maintain a list of TODOs, in-progress, and completed tasks. (use GitHub's default statuses)
* Make updates weekly, or at the minimum every two weeks. (commits + issues)
* Start to develop your own way of effectively managing your time and productivity!
:::

Imagine you are working in a team of 10--25 engineers. By having your tasks and thoughts organized your team lead (i.e., Prof Wallace) and other members of your team (i.e., the other students in this class) can easily help or collaborate with you. This is super important in remote or hybrid jobs. Seeing commit history and task updates make it easier to know someone is putting forth an honest effort.

### Collaboration and Helping your Peers

During the semester collaborate with a classmate and complete at least 3 issues for 3 different projects.

:::tip[Goals]
In `collab.md` provide:
* The project name and your classmate's name.
* A link to the project task/issue in GitHub. Or, a link to what helped them with.
* A 1--2 sentence reflection of the problem and how you helped solve it.
:::

If you having trouble finding someone to collaborate with, let me know! Or here are some options:
* Ask in the class in EdStem.
* Complete someone's survey.
* Complete someone's study or pilot study.
* Review someone's repository and see if you can help them.
    * Review their code and provide feedback. (be nice!)
    * Can you get their system running on your machine? Provide feedback on their instructions.


## Your GitHub Repository

A quick few goals and must-haves for your GitHub repository. Remember, because we are in a GitHub org, everyone in the class has access! So no need to make the repository public.

:::tip[Goals]
* A README.md with instructions for how to run the code locally.
* The README.md should also have instructions for how to run the code in production.
* Assign a software license. (Prof Wallace will cover these in class)
* A link to access a working version of your code. (if this is not possible talk with Prof Wallace)
:::

These are essential steps to help others understand and run your code. How many good open source projects have bad README files? Not many. ;)

If you need help forking your repo from your lab let me know. If you have any questions or concerns please let me know! :)
